# On the grid the speed is held and the output comes from the steam; off the grid the load is a resisting torque

**Status: accepted.** First decided in #71 (the island model), speed modes amended in #135. **Rewritten in place on 2026-11-28** for Electrical M1 ([ADR 0046](0046-from-island-to-town-grid-in-milestones.md)) after the grilling of the [M1 research note](../research/2026-11-grid-m1.md); built by #201 (stiff grid and MW controller), #202 (reverse power and the sequential trip) and #203 (auto-sync). **Until those land the code still follows the island model** (below, "Before M1"). M1 goes ahead before the tube model as the one exception of [ADR 0057](0057-boiler-as-a-tube-model.md).

With the generator breaker closed the unit is on a **stiff grid**: the grid holds the shaft at sync speed, the steam decides the MW, and a MW controller moves the valve to the load the operator asks for. With the breaker open (house load operation, or no load) the shaft is free and the generator load is a resisting torque, as before.

## Why

In the island model the speed sagged under load, the Hold PID opened the valve, and a lack of steam ended in the breaker opening on a −2 rpm sag. On a real grid the speed is set by the system (a 150 MW unit moves the Continental European frequency by about 8 mHz, 0.5 rpm, while the grid itself wanders ±50 mHz: research note, section 5), so a lack of steam costs MW, never speed. The island model was chosen because a pinned speed gave the speed PID no error to act on; a MW controller is the real answer to that.

## Decisions

- **Stiff grid (#201):** while the generator breaker is closed the shaft runs at exactly `SyncRPM` (3000 rpm); the swing equation is not solved. No grid inertia, frequency, droop or frequency bias in M1 (all zero at a fixed 50 Hz; they come with M3).
- **Electric output from the steam (#201):** while synchronised, `electricOutput = (steam shaft power − shaft loss at 3000 rpm) × GeneratorEfficiency`. It may go **negative**: the generator motors. The shaft loss stays sized on `NoLoadFlowFraction` (3 %), which gives a motoring power of about 3 % of rating, the top of the sourced 0.5 to 3 %; #201 measures it.
- **MW controller (#201)** replaces the speed PID while synchronised: a **pure integral** controller on the turbine valve, `Ki = 0.05 pu/pu/s` (a 20 s reset; sourced range 0.005 to 0.2, IEEE PES-TR1 LCFB1 and NERC 2019), on (load reference − electric output), bumpless from the valve position at the close, saturating at a fully open valve. Hand valve steps are refused while synchronised.
- **Load setpoint and load reference:** the setpoint is the operator's MW value, default 0, limited to 0 up to `LoadSetpointLimit` (200 MW), only settable with the breaker closed (`loadSetpointRefusal`), ignored while the grid supply breaker is open (ADR 0053, #174) and while the vital bus is dead (ADR 0055). The **load reference** ramps toward it at `LoadRate`, 3 % of rated output per minute, up and down (sourced from coal units, [research note](../research/2026-11-generator-load-rate.md); inside every range of the M1 note); the MW controller follows the reference. No operator-set rate (M5). With the vital bus dead the controller keeps working toward its last reference.
- **Boiler-follow by hand:** the valve follows MW, the operator is the boiler master and holds the pressure with the gas. A load raise dips the pressure at once. An automatic boiler master is a later item, after the tube model. No pressure limiter or runback: with too little steam the valve goes fully open and the pressure falls to the low-pressure closure (10 bar, [ADR 0026](0026-valve-flow-follows-pressure.md)); real units have an initial pressure limiter, a gap in the register.
- **Closing the breaker (#203):** no phase in M1 (phase, the ±10° window, the closing time and the advance angle come with the synchroscope of M4); the voltage counts as matched. The close window is a slip of **0 to +4 rpm** (0 to +0.067 Hz, IEEE C50.13, generator faster than the grid), for the hand close and the auto-sync alike, like a sync-check supervising both; outside it the close is refused with a permissive lamp ([ADR 0054](0054-status-by-lamps-not-text.md)). The breaker closes instantly once the window is met. Load at the close is 0 MW (no basic load, #139).
- **Auto-sync (#203):** a turn switch ([ADR 0039](0039-latching-controls-are-turn-switches.md)), allowed only in Hold at the 3000 rpm run-up point; it raises the speed reference to 3000 + 3 rpm (0.05 Hz, inside the sourced 0.02 to 0.1 Hz), closes the breaker once the slip is in the window and turns itself off. With the vital bus dead it ignores commands and a sync in progress stops.
- **Opening the breaker:** by hand or by reverse power only; the generator breaker has **no speed auto-open** (with the speed held it cannot lose synchronism in M1). The **unit aux breaker keeps its ±2 rpm auto-open** (`BreakerRPMMargin`) for house load operation, where the shaft is free; it needs no bus. On any opening the load, the reference and the setpoint drop to 0, the synchronised mode ends in Hold at 3000 rpm (#135) and the shaft is free from that tick: a load rejection.
- **Reverse power (#202):** pickup at minus half the sim's own motoring power (about 2 to 2.5 MW; SEL citing IEEE C37.102). Two stages: a **sequential stage of 3 s**, armed only while the turbine is tripped, which opens the generator breaker (Basler's figure, a search snippet only: an estimate in the register); a **backup stage of 20 s** (inside ABB's sourced 5 to 30 s), for motoring without a turbine trip, which trips the turbine with reverse power as a new cause and opens the breaker. No new latch: the turbine reset of [ADR 0043](0043-trip-reset.md) covers it, and reverse power has no persistent cause. It **needs control power**: with the safety bus dead it cannot open the breaker and the generator keeps motoring ([ADR 0055](0055-the-vital-bus-and-the-safety-bus.md)). Annunciator: a **REVERSE POWER** tile, lit when either stage opens the breaker.
- **Every turbine trip is sequential (#202):** vacuum, low lube oil, boiler high-high and the low-pressure closure (there are no electrical fault trips that would open the breaker at once). The valve closes, the speed stays held, the output goes negative and the sequential stage opens the breaker; the shaft then coasts down with no steam.
- **A lost load is caught by the speed controls:** after the breaker opens, Hold at 3000 rpm pulls the shaft back; past `MaxRPM` the overspeed trip acts ([ADR 0040](0040-turbine-trip-on-overspeed.md)). #201 re-checks this with a full-load rejection; an overspeed trip there goes back to the owner.
- **Off the grid the island model stays.** With the generator breaker open the shaft balance is `J·dω/dt = drive torque − loss torque − load torque`, with `load torque = (load / GeneratorEfficiency) / ω`. In house load operation (generator breaker open, unit aux breaker closed, #189) the auxiliary load is that load, from the tick the generator breaker opens, with no ramp, and the setpoint is 0 (#174, #182). With the generator breaker closed the auxiliary load stays inside the gross output, with no torque of its own (ADR 0053).
- **Excitation and reactive power are out of M1:** no AVR, MVA, power factor, capability curve or loss-of-field protection; the operator sees MW only. M4 decides when voltage comes back (a voltage knob beside the speed knob of the synchroscope).
- **Speed mode with the breaker closed (#135):** closing the breaker selects the **synchronised** mode; the operator cannot select a speed mode or a run-up point while it is closed (one constant refusal reason). The mode ends when the breaker opens (to Hold at 3000) or on a trip (to Free).

## Before M1 (the code until #201 to #203 land)

The speed is free with the breaker closed; the load is a resisting torque; the synchronised mode is the Hold PID aimed at 3000 rpm; the electric output is the load carried, moving at `LoadRate` toward the setpoint, never derived from the steam; closing is allowed within ±2 rpm either side, and both breakers auto-open when the speed leaves that window; a trip or a lack of steam ends at that auto-open. There is no reverse power and no auto-sync.

## Considered options

- **Speed free with the breaker closed (the island model, #71 to M1):** replaced. It made a lack of steam cost speed instead of MW, and opened the breaker on a 2 rpm sag. It stays for house load operation.
- **A large finite grid inertia in M1:** rejected for M1. By the research note's figures the speed would move by under 1 rpm, invisible in M1; a moving frequency is M3's town grid.
- **Valve open-loop from the load reference with a ±5 % MW trim:** rejected. MW would simply follow the pressure; the integral controller gives the boiler-follow behaviour the sources describe and leaves the pressure to the operator.
- **An automatic boiler master (boiler-follow, turbine-follow or coordinated) in M1:** deferred. Its tuning depends on the boiler dynamics, which the tube model changes (ADR 0057).
- **One reverse power delay:** rejected; real relays separate the planned sequential trip (short) from unplanned motoring (long).
- **Removing the hand close until M4:** rejected; a sync-check supervises hand and automatic closing alike.
- **A load controller with frequency bias, droop, or a scripted demand profile:** M3 and later.

## Consequences

- `TurbineState` gains the load reference and the MW controller's integral state; `electricOutput` is computed from the steam while synchronised and may be negative. Goldens and snapshots move (`turbine-load`, `max-output`, `load-rejection`, `turbine-sync`).
- New register rows in [design-basis.md](../plant/design-basis.md), each added by the ticket that builds it: the stiff grid, Ki 0.05, the initial pressure limiter as a gap, the reverse power pickup, the 3 s estimate and the 20 s delay, the +3 rpm auto-sync target and the 0 to +4 rpm window.
- The ±50 mHz (±3 rpm) normal wander of a real grid is wider than the unit aux breaker's ±2 rpm; M3 replaces the speed auto-open by under- and over-frequency protection (ANSI 81).
