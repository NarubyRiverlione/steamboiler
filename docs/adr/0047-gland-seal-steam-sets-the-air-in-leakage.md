# Gland seal steam sets the air in-leakage

**Status: accepted.** Decided in the #147 grilling. Amends [ADR 0035](0035-condenser-pressure-is-vapour-plus-air.md), whose single constant in-leakage becomes two rates selected by the gland seal. Built in #147.

## The decision

- **A switch and a flag.** Gland seal steam is an operator on/off switch (a latching control, [ADR 0039](0039-latching-controls-are-turn-switches.md)). The seal is a state flag: sealed while the steam is on, unsealed otherwise. No steam valve, no seal pressure, no lag.
- **Two leak rates, no lag.** The air in-leakage is the sealed residual rate (`AirLeakRate` today, 0.0022 kg/s) while sealed, and a sourced, larger open-gland rate while unsealed. The leak follows the flag at once. Both rates are sourced in a research note; an unsourced figure is marked UNVERIFIED and recorded in `design-basis.md` as a deliberate choice, never inflated to force a stall. The pump is the real size since #165 ([ADR 0035](0035-condenser-pressure-is-vapour-plus-air.md)), so the open-gland leak is not inflated either: it is a labyrinth estimate, and the stall follows from it.
- **The rotor must turn.** Gland steam is admitted only while the rotor turns: the turning gear engaged ([#146](https://github.com/NarubyRiverlione/steamboiler/issues/146)) or rpm above the standstill threshold (`CstTurningGear.StandstillRpm`). Steam on a stationary rotor bows it. The reducer refuses the admission otherwise (the state does not change); the Turbine aux page disables the switch and says why ([#164](https://github.com/NarubyRiverlione/steamboiler/issues/164)). If the rotor stops turning while the steam is on, the steam is shut off and the seal is lost (the gear out and the rpm at or below the threshold); the plant keeps the shut-off in `glandSteamShutOff` (#177) until the steam is admitted again: set only by this automatic shut-off, never by the operator switch, cleared by `SET_GLAND_STEAM` on. It lights the annunciator tile "Gland steam shut off" ([ADR 0054](0054-status-by-lamps-not-text.md)), so the operator sees it on any page. The roll keeps the steam: the gear stays engaged while the steam is admitted and throws itself out above the standstill speed, so the rotor is never stopped on the way ([ADR 0051](0051-turning-gear-holds-the-stopped-rotor-and-blocks-the-valve.md)). Turning gear alone is not the condition: after the gear has thrown itself out, the turbine runs on gland steam.
- **A small boiler draw, returned to the hotwell.** The gland steam is a fixed flow (`CstGlandSteam.Flow`, 0.1 kg/s, UNVERIFIED) taken from the boiler and returned to the hotwell through the gland condenser, so the water invariant holds. The draw does not wait for boiler pressure, and is limited only to the steam the drum holds (the same mass returns): a real plant uses its auxiliary steam header on a cold start (not modelled, recorded in the design basis). The return arrives as condensate at the condenser outlet temperature; the small heat of the steam is not tracked.
- **Start state.** The Cold state starts with gland steam off. Every scenario that starts hot or running sets it on explicitly, so their vacuum and snapshots do not change by accident.

- **The figures.** Sealed `AirLeakRate` 0.0022 kg/s (sourced); open `OpenGlandAirLeakRate` 0.14 kg/s (a Stodola labyrinth estimate for two LP glands with assumed geometry, UNVERIFIED, #165); with the glands open the 0.3 m³/s air pump settles at 210 kg of air (416 mbar with the vapour) and the SJAE never joins (suction limit 170 mbar), with them sealed the pump alone holds about 6 mbar of air and the SJAE 2 mbar. See [the research note](../research/2026-11-gland-seal-steam.md).
- **Where it lives.** `glandSteamOn` and `glandSteamShutOff` in `TurbineState` (the glands are turbine hardware), set by `SET_GLAND_STEAM`, shut off in the plant tick (`GlandSteamTick`). The seal flag is not a second field: the glands are sealed while the switch is on, and `calculateAirInventory` takes it as an argument.

## Considered options

- **Condition on the gear only (the ticket's first wording):** rejected. The glands would lose their seal exactly when the turbine rolls.
- **A main-steam-valve exception to the shut-off:** tried, rejected. It left steam on a stationary rotor while the valve was open; the cause was the disengage-then-roll rule, which ADR 0051 dropped.
- **Raising the leak until it stalls the real pump:** rejected by the realism rule. The stall needs about 0.108 kg/s against the 0.3 m³/s pump; the 0.14 kg/s used is the labyrinth estimate, not a value picked for the stall. The earlier 0.0347 kg/s (60 SCFM) left the glands without a meaning: 120 mbar open with the pump alone.
- **A gland steam valve and a seal pressure:** rejected for now. The ticket needs sealed or unsealed; a seal pressure has no source.
- **A lag on the seal:** rejected. A time constant with no source.

## Out of scope

- Losing the seal on a turbine trip: **researched in #161 and not built.** No source says a trip cuts the sealing steam; the header is fed from auxiliary or main steam ahead of the control valves and a trip does not touch it ([research](../research/2026-11-gland-seal-on-trip.md)). A trip keeps the seal; the operator's gland steam switch and the stopped-rotor rule remain the only ways to lose it. A faster vacuum collapse after a trip would come from the vacuum breaker (own ticket) or a lost auxiliary steam supply (needs-triage), not from the seal.
